## Google-Style AI For Science / Research Engineering Note

Dear hiring team,

I am a PhD computational scientist interested in research-engineering work at the intersection of scientific computing, AI evaluation, and evidence-backed technical communication. My recent public work is centered on source-first workflows: small tools with explicit manifests, schema checks, tests, dry-run plans, and review packets that make numerical and visualization outputs easier to inspect.

The clearest example is VisualBasilisk, a public Basilisk VOF/facet-to-Blender bridge designed around reproducibility and conservative claim boundaries. I also maintain public project pages and evidence packets for Basilisk, OpenFOAM, DualSPHysics, and reduced jet-model communication. These artifacts are intentionally careful: they separate exploratory visualization from stronger scientific claims and document what evidence is missing before any stronger conclusion.

I would bring a combination of computational physics judgment, implementation discipline, and public-safe communication to teams building or evaluating AI-for-science workflows.

## Alignerr-Style CFD / Domain Evaluation Note

Dear evaluation team,

I am a CFD/fluid-mechanics domain reviewer with hands-on experience around Basilisk VOF, OpenFOAM, DualSPHysics/SPH, scientific visualization, and reduced jet-model communication. I evaluate AI-generated scientific answers by checking governing assumptions, boundary conditions, nondimensional scales, numerical-method limits, field availability, and whether the visual evidence actually supports the claim.

I have prepared self-authored CFD evaluator samples and rubrics that show this style of review without using proprietary or live-platform materials. The examples cover unsupported VOF breakup claims, zero-range pressure visualizations, imposed-inlet versus pressure-driven boundary-condition mismatches, convergence schedule alignment, and dimensionality limits in visualization demos.

My evaluation style is direct: identify the claim, classify what is supported, provide safe corrected wording, and specify the exact evidence needed for a stronger answer.

## Tailoring Checklist

- Name the role only after checking the actual posting.
- Reference only public or human-approved evidence.
- Keep company-specific claims out unless independently true.
- Replace generic project lists with the two most relevant examples.
- Keep boundaries visible but not defensive.
- Do not imply submission, acceptance, employment, certification, validation, or production readiness.
