## About Variant A: Scientific AI / Research Engineering

I am a PhD computational scientist working across scientific computing, CFD/fluid mechanics, visualization, and AI evaluation. My recent public work focuses on source-first workflows: small tools, explicit manifests, tests, dry-run plans, evidence packets, and conservative documentation that make numerical outputs easier to inspect without overstating what they prove.

Recent examples include VisualBasilisk, a Basilisk VOF/facet-to-Blender bridge with synthetic fixtures and CLI checks, plus public CFD visualization pages for Basilisk, OpenFOAM, DualSPHysics, and reduced jet-model communication. These projects are intentionally modest: they show workflow design, review discipline, and technical communication, not production CFD or experimental validation.

I am strongest in roles that need domain rigor around AI-for-science, scientific workflow tooling, simulation evidence review, and public-safe technical explanation.

## About Variant B: CFD / Domain Evaluation

I review scientific-computing and CFD outputs by asking what the evidence actually supports. That means checking governing assumptions, boundary conditions, nondimensional scales, mesh and timestep limits, field availability, visualization artifacts, and claim boundaries.

My recent work includes public-safe CFD evaluator samples, Basilisk and OpenFOAM review artifacts, VisualBasilisk tooling, and site pages that separate exploratory visualization from stronger physics claims. I focus on catching subtle errors: missing pressure fields, unsupported atomisation language, station/time convergence mismatch, one-cell debris, projection artifacts, and imposed-inlet cases mislabeled as internal-nozzle evidence.

I am interested in scientific AI evaluation and research-engineering roles where careful technical judgment is as important as producing the artifact.
